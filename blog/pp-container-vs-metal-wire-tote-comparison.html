PP Containers vs. Metal and Wire Totes: A Procurement Comparison

Polypropylenecontainer Editorial • October 2026

Choosing the Right Reusable Container Material

Procurement teams standardizing a reusable container fleet usually narrow the field to a few material options: polypropylene, steel or galvanized wire, and occasionally aluminum. Each has a legitimate place in industrial logistics, but the differences in total cost of ownership, hygiene performance, and handling ergonomics are significant enough that choosing the wrong material can lock an operation into years of avoidable cost. This comparison looks at the practical trade-offs so buyers can match the material to the application.

The headline distinction is simple: metal totes are engineered for extreme point loads and abrasive environments, while polypropylene containers from Elipacko are engineered for sanitation, weight efficiency, and favorable economics across the broad middle of industrial and food-handling use cases. Understanding where your application falls on that spectrum is the key to a sound decision.

Weight and Handling Economics

Weight is where polypropylene posts its clearest win. A wire or steel tote can weigh three to five times more than a comparable PP container. That difference compounds across every touchpoint in your supply chain. Heavier containers mean higher freight cost on every outbound and return leg, more worker fatigue and injury risk during manual handling, and reduced net payload per trailer when tare weight eats into gross vehicle limits.

  • Freight: Lower tare weight directly reduces transportation spend, especially in closed-loop systems where empties are shipped back.
  • Ergonomics: Lighter containers lower the risk of lifting injuries and the associated workers compensation exposure.
  • Payload: Every pound of container weight is a pound of product you cannot ship in weight-constrained lanes.

For operations that move containers frequently or ship returns over long distances, the cumulative freight savings from polypropylene often dwarf any difference in unit purchase price.

Hygiene and Compliance

In food, pharmaceutical, and other regulated environments, sanitation is non-negotiable, and this is a decisive advantage for polypropylene. PP has a smooth, non-porous surface that does not corrode, rust, or harbor bacteria in seams and welds the way metal and wire structures can. It withstands aggressive wash chemistry, high-temperature sanitation, and repeated cleaning cycles without degrading. Containers molded from PP turnover boxes specifications can be produced in FDA-compliant resin for direct food contact, which is difficult or impossible to certify with many coated metal products once the coating wears.

Wire totes, by contrast, present numerous welds and crevices that complicate validated cleaning, and galvanized coatings can flake over time, creating both contamination and corrosion concerns. For any operation subject to audit, the documentation burden of proving a metal tote clean is consistently higher than for molded polypropylene.

Durability and Failure Modes

Metal does hold an edge in raw impact and abrasion resistance, which matters in heavy foundry, metal-stamping, or scrap applications where containers take severe punishment. In these niches, steel remains the right call. For the vast majority of distribution, food processing, and general industrial applications, however, modern polypropylene delivers more than enough durability to reach hundreds of trips. PP also fails more gracefully: it does not develop sharp rusted edges, and when a unit does reach end of life it is fully recyclable rather than destined for scrap-metal handling.

Making the Call

The decision framework is straightforward. If your application involves extreme point loads, abrasive scrap, or sustained high heat beyond polypropylene limits, metal earns its place. For everything else, including the large majority of food, beverage, pharmaceutical, and general distribution work, polypropylene usually wins on total cost of ownership once freight, labor, hygiene compliance, and recyclability are weighed together.
